Exploration

Off the map, off the grid, off the planet

Solo Sailor Attempts First-Ever Nonstop, Unassisted Arctic Ocean Circumnavigation

Matt Rutherford — founder of the nonprofit Ocean Research Project and a two-time Guinness World Record holder — departed Aasiaat, Greenland in late June aboard his 42-foot sailboat, attempting to become the first person to circle the Arctic Ocean solo, nonstop, and unassisted. The 10,000-plus-mile route threads the North Atlantic, Russia's Northern Sea Route, and Canada's Northwest Passage — three of the most hostile ocean environments on Earth. It's only attemptable now because Arctic sea ice this summer sits 9-15% below the previous record low. Rutherford, sailing via Starlink, gives himself 75% odds of finishing by his early-October target and says his biggest uncertainty isn't the ice — it's simple exhaustion from snatching 30 minutes of sleep at a time once the 24-hour daylight fades.

Source: GeoGarage / Ocean Research Project

SpaceX Hits 100th Launch of the Year — Third Year Running

SpaceX launched its 100th mission of 2026 before dawn Wednesday, sending 24 more Starlink satellites to orbit from Vandenberg — the company's third straight year cracking the century mark, with roughly three-quarters of this year's launches being Starlink runs. The same 24 hours brought two other notable space stories: China's LandSpace became the first commercial Chinese company to land an orbital-class rocket booster (using landing legs, a first for a Chinese-made vehicle), and NASA astronaut Anil Menon and ESA's Sophie Adenot — now the first French woman to spacewalk — had to remove a failed ISS communications antenna and ran out of time to install its replacement, meaning a return trip outside is now on the schedule.

Source: Space.com

Golden Globe Race Fleet Prepares for September 6 Start — Sextants Only

The 2026 Golden Globe Race — the retro-format solo circumnavigation that bans GPS, autopilots and modern electronics in favor of sextants and paper charts — starts September 6. Among the entrants: Erden Eruç, already the most experienced solo ocean rower alive (26,705 miles rowed, 1,084 solo days at sea), who bought a 1976 Biscay 36 to enter the race in the old-school spirit its founders intended. It's a different flavor of modern exploration than the satellite-tracked speed record attempts — a deliberate throwback to Robin Knox-Johnston's original 1968 solo non-stop circumnavigation.

On This Day

August 19, through the centuries
1812 — War

The USS Constitution defeated the British frigate HMS Guerriere off Nova Scotia in a 30-minute engagement. Witnesses claimed British cannonballs bounced off the Constitution's oak hull "as if built of iron" — earning the ship its enduring nickname, "Old Ironsides," and handing the young United States Navy a huge morale victory early in the War of 1812.

1934 — Decision

A national plebiscite in Germany approved consolidating the offices of Chancellor and President into a single role vested in Adolf Hitler, formalizing the "Führer" title and removing the last major check on his authority — a stark example of how a single ballot measure can reshape a nation's entire trajectory.

1960 — Discovery

The Soviet Union launched Sputnik 5, sending the dogs Belka and Strelka, a rabbit, 40 mice, two rats and assorted fruit flies into orbit — and, critically, bringing them all back alive. It was the first spacecraft to carry living passengers into orbit and return them safely, directly paving the way for Yuri Gagarin's human spaceflight less than a year later.

1964 — Discovery

NASA launched Syncom 3, the world's first geostationary communications satellite, from Cape Canaveral. Parking a satellite in a fixed spot relative to Earth's surface was the breakthrough that made continuous live intercontinental television broadcast possible — Syncom 3 carried the 1964 Tokyo Olympics to American TV sets that October.

1991 — Decision

Communist hardliners placed Soviet leader Mikhail Gorbachev under house arrest in an attempted coup. It collapsed within two days in the face of public resistance led by Boris Yeltsin, but the attempt fatally undermined Gorbachev's authority and accelerated the dissolution of the USSR just four months later.
